Risks and Adverse Health Effects of Methylene Blue+

It is not safe to use methylene blue. When used in conjunction with certain opioids and serotonergic medications, the FDA-approved IV medicine contains a boxed warning for potentially deadly or serious serotonin syndrome. Due to the possibility of hemolytic anaemia, it is also not recommended for those who have a deficiency in glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase, or G6PD.

Additional adverse effects could include headache, nausea, dizziness, confusion, changes in skin or urine colour, and allergic reactions. The margin between potentially beneficial pharmacological benefits and toxicity of methylene blue is thin, and greater concentrations are especially concerning. A person's health issues, drugs, dosage, and formulation can all affect risk.

The quality of the goods is another issue. A prescription medication cannot be used in place of Methylene Blue+ Total Health. Online and pharmacist-sold products are not always the same. Customers may be subjected to different manufacturing standards, formulations, concentrations, and labelling techniques. Industrial or laboratory-grade methylene blue should not be regarded as a medication or nutritional supplement.
